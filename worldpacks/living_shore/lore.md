# The Living Shore — Lore Bible (Game Master reference)

The Living Shore is a beach that exists between universes. It is real, it
is old, and it is patient. A great ribbon of pale light — **the Beam** —
runs above the tideline at dusk, and standing in the surf beneath it are
**doors**: free-standing portals, each leading to a different universe.
Nobody knows who built the doors. The doors do not say.

## The rules of the Shore

- **The Lantern Law**: open flame is sacred on the Shore. A lit lantern is
  a promise that you are a person, not a hunger. Things that mean harm
  cannot carry light; they carry the memory of light instead.
- **The Campfire Truce**: around the driftwood fire at Driftwood Camp, all
  travelers are temporarily equal and unarmed in spirit. Fights do not
  start there; debts are spoken, not collected.
- **The tide is a clock.** High tide hides; low tide reveals. The sea cave
  and the Marrow Bell wreck only surface when the tide is low.
- **Doors are one-way until they aren't.** A door always lets you out.
  What it lets out may not be what walked in.
- **Names have weight here.** To know a thing's true name is to hold its
  leash. Sensitive souls feel this the way others feel weather.

## Places

**The Tide Gate** is where the Beam touches the shore and the doors stand
in the shallows, worn like sea-stones. A weathered signpost reads: *Dada
Chunk, Da Dachink — Open a Door Before You Sink.* Nobody admits to writing
it. Everyone obeys it.

**Driftwood Camp** circles a fire that never drowns. Old Cardo, the last
door-tender, keeps it. He remembers every door that ever opened here, and
charges no coin — only stories.

**The Salt & Lantern** is a travelers' refuge that is warm from the inside
no matter the weather. Maren, the glass-eyed tavernkeeper, pours something
dark and fails to answer most questions honestly. Her rumors are better
currency than her beer.

**The Skyless Light** is the abandoned lighthouse on the point. Its lamp
room is empty, its stairs are worn by feet that no longer exist, and on
storm nights the locals say it sweeps the water with a beam that casts no
shadow. It was not always abandoned. It may not be now.

**The sea cave and the Marrow Bell wreck** belong to the low tide. The
wreck of the *Marrow Bell* — a salvage sloop that hauled things up instead
of hauling them across — still carries her bronze bell, and her brass is
still good.

## Tone

Dread like deep water: quiet, patient, more melancholy than cruel. Wonders
are real and so are the costs. The Shore wants travelers to succeed — the
doors need people to walk through them — but the sea does not care.
